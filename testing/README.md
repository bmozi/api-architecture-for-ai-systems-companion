# Independent-Practitioner Test Packet 01

## Status and evidence boundary

**Status:** Prepared for use; no session has been conducted.

This packet tests whether an independent practitioner can use the blank API
Meaning-and-Authority Brief and API/Event/Workflow Decision Tree without the
book, a completed example, or architecture coaching. It is designed to expose
misinterpretation, skipped decisions, and unsafe recovery—not to demonstrate
that the participant agrees with the book's vocabulary.

The test scenario and every organization, system, event, time boundary, and
incident in it are fictional. The packet is a proposed usability method. Until
a session is run and its evidence is retained, it establishes no result about
reader comprehension, practitioner performance, architecture quality,
production safety, or business value.

“Blinded” here means the participant does not receive the completed Northbridge
or Harborline examples, the facilitator prompts, the observation rubric, the
scoring rubric, or an answer key. It does not mean this is a randomized,
controlled, or formally validated research study.

## Primary questions

The packet asks whether a practitioner can, without coaching:

- state the consumer-facing promise and its limits;
- separate identity from permission to act for someone else;
- distinguish a transport response from a business decision;
- keep a repeated or timed-out request from creating a second business effect;
- assign responsibility when work outlives the initial interaction;
- describe what other systems may safely infer from a notification;
- reconcile disagreement across participating mechanisms; and
- identify missing facts rather than filling them with confident invention.

The facilitator also records whether an initial mistake is corrected
spontaneously, after a neutral prompt, only after a definition, or not at all.

## Running the packet

Freeze the versions of the scenario, workbook, and two tools before recruiting
a participant. Record file hashes or another immutable version identifier in
the results log. Run one participant at a time. Preserve negative results and
facilitator interventions.

Do not revise the tools during a session. After the session, classify each
issue as likely tool wording, scenario ambiguity, participant interpretation,
facilitator contamination, or unresolved. One participant may expose a severe
defect; one participant cannot establish broad usability.
